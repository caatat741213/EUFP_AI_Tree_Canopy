Problem Statement:

Cities such as Kitchener, Waterloo, and Cambridge invest in urban tree planting and maintenance, yet limited forestry resources may not always be prioritized according to environmental need, tree asset condition, and equity outcomes. As a result, neighbourhoods with lower canopy coverage and greater exposure to heat and environmental stress may continue to receive less targeted support.
Although municipal forestry teams have access to tree inventory and canopy datasets, they lack a unified, reproducible framework for identifying which streets or neighbourhoods should be prioritized for planting or maintenance. This creates a gap between available data and location-specific decision-making.
This project addresses that gap by developing a data-informed prioritization model, presented through an interactive map-based decision-support tool, to help planners identify areas where tree planting or maintenance could deliver the greatest combined environmental, social, and asset-management benefit. This will also help municipal forestry teams to allocate limited budgets more efficiently, reducing resource waste, eliminating neighbourhood bias, and ensuring that underserved communities receive equitable access to the environmental benefits of urban tree coverage.
